Command Center

A commerce command center that connects customer behavior, product interest, marketing activity, and store performance to the decisions teams make every day.

Owned
Concept, design, integration
Users
5 teams, from clienteling to leadership
Period
Past 60 days

Why it was needed

Commerce, marketing and retail information lived in different systems. By the time a report arrived, the opportunity had passed.

The trigger: customer retention was declining while average order value stayed flat. The shoppers most likely to buy again were browsing, hesitating and leaving, and nobody saw it happen.

How teams worked before
  • Clienteling learned a VIP had been browsing only after they had left, and reached out without seeing what they looked at.
  • Merchandising found out demand outran inventory once a piece had already sold out.
  • Marketing judged an email, SMS or ad by next-day reports, with creative and landing-page effects mixed together.
  • Stores and online looked at separate numbers, so event days and launches had no shared view.
  • Leadership pieced together the morning picture from several tools, and tagging problems went unnoticed.

Who uses it

  • Private Client Sales and clienteling
  • E-commerce and merchandising
  • Marketing
  • Retail stores
  • Leadership and operations

What I owned

1

Concept

With retention declining and AOV flat, I proposed scoring and monitoring shoppers in a live 3D simulation of the store, so teams could act while a customer was still browsing.

2

Design

Organized the product around decisions each team makes, not around data sources.

3

Integration

Connected the Shopify API, the RVO API, Meta Ads through MCP and Google Ads through MCP, so every shopper carries their orders, value and the campaign that brought them.

Key decisions

A store floor, not another dashboardStaff already think in collections and zones. Showing shoppers walking through the Galleria lets anyone read the moment at a glance, without reading a report.
Live, not next-dayThe core view covers the last 30 minutes: who is inside, who is carrying a bag, who is at checkout. A follow-up is worth most while the shopper is still deciding.
Score the shopper, not the sessionEach shopper card joins lifetime value, order count, average order and where they are now, so teams can rank who deserves attention first.
Tie every visit to its campaignPulling Meta and Google Ads in through MCP links a shopper to the exact campaign that brought them, separating ad quality from on-site behavior.
Organize by decision, not by data source36 use cases were grouped into five team decisions, so each team opens the view to a question it already asks every day.
Anonymous by default, alerts over watchingShoppers show as guests until staff search for a specific client. Surges, drop-offs and contested pieces raise alerts, so nobody has to stare at the screen.

What it enables

36 documented use cases, grouped by the decision each team makes.

Client action

  • Spot a VIP who is active right now
  • Review a client’s recent footprint before outreach
  • Catch high-intent drop-off and recover a bag
  • Connect visits, email, product interest and purchases

Merchandising action

  • Detect demand that may exceed inventory
  • Compare brand attention and dwell time
  • Understand cross-shopping paths and search gaps
  • Forecast demand before a drop

Marketing action

  • Watch traffic after an email or SMS send
  • Compare Meta and Google traffic quality
  • Find high-intent retargeting audiences
  • Separate creative performance from landing-page performance

Retail action

  • See store sales as they happen
  • Compare store and online activity
  • Monitor event days
  • Give staff a shared view of activity

Leadership action

  • Review the morning business pulse
  • Understand international demand and shipping destinations
  • Replay recent activity for training and demos
  • Find data-quality and tagging problems
